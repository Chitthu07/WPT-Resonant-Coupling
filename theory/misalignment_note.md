# A-S2 lateral misalignment — note  (design geometry, params v1-design)

Generated by `python -m sim.misalignment` (or `python -m sim.run_ab`). The secondary is at axial
spacing d and shifted sideways by dx. Efficiency and load power come from Member B's phasor solver
`sim/circuit.py` `solve(...)` at f0 = 500 kHz. Design circuit values from `sim/params.py`:
L = 17.13 uH, C = 5.915 nF, R1 = R2 = 0.455 ohm (C-T2 budget with computed proximity loss, 2026-10-25), Vs = 5 V peak, Rs = 0 ohm (ideal source).
k uses the filament-sum L = 17.128 uH. At f0 the solver agrees with the closed-form eta to 1e-9 (pytest).

Two fixed loads are compared:
- **opt0**: R_L = R_L,opt at dx = 0 for each d (CONVENTIONS.md default): 11.70 ohm at d = 3 cm, 6.00 ohm at d = 5 cm.
- **team**: R_L = 6.0 ohm, the fixed load chosen in C-C1 (`params.RL_fixed`, = R_L,opt at d = 5 cm to 0.07 %), from `sim.link.fixed_load()`.
  This is the load B uses for eta(d) and P_L(d). At d = 5 cm the two loads are the same to 0.07 % (5.996 vs 6.0 ohm).

- Efficiency stays within 2 points of its aligned value up to dx ≈ 2 cm and within about 9 points up to dx ≈ 4 cm
  (about 0.8 coil radius), then collapses as k^2 Q1 Q2 approaches 1.
- With the team load, d = 3 cm is slightly *less* efficient at dx = 0 (91.0 % vs 92.5 %) but degrades more slowly.
  6 ohm is below the optimum load for d = 3 cm, and R_L,opt falls as k falls, so the fixed load gets closer to the
  optimum as the coils slide apart.
- Beyond dx ≈ 6.9 cm, M at d = 5 cm is larger than M at d = 3 cm (0.404 vs 0.383 uH at 7 cm). The primary's field
  spreads out with height, so a more distant secondary still catches flux at large offsets.

## Load power rises with misalignment (ideal source)

With the ideal 5 V source (Rs = 0), P_L **increases** as the coils misalign. It peaks at about 6.3 W near dx ≈ 6.5 cm,
compared with 0.5 W (d = 3 cm) and 1.8 W (d = 5 cm) when aligned. This is the over-coupled regime from B-S1. At f0 the
secondary reflects a resistance w0^2 M^2 / (R2 + R_L) into the primary. When aligned, this reflected resistance is
large and limits I1. As M falls, I1 rises toward Vs / R1 = 11.0 A peak. P_L is largest where k falls to the critical
coupling k_c, i.e. w0^2 M^2 = (R1 + Rs)(R2 + R_L). Beyond that, P_L falls again.

Consequence: at large offsets the primary current reaches about 9 to 11 A peak, and the primary coil would dissipate
about ½ · 11.0^2 · 0.455 ≈ 27.5 W. This is a property of the ideal-source model. A real 50 ohm function generator
limits I1 to below Vs / 50 ohm = 0.1 A. The project follows the no-hardware roadmap, so this case is kept only as a
simulation point (B-C1 stress table) showing why an ideal source is unrealistic. With Rs = 50 ohm the P_L(d) curve
has a completely different shape (peak near d = 2 cm instead of rising to 10 cm, see `fig_PL_vs_d`), so any P_L number
must always be quoted together with its Rs.

## Sign change of M

M crosses zero at **dx = 8.22 cm for d = 3 cm** and at **dx = 9.97 cm for d = 5 cm**, and is negative beyond that.

**Physical reason.** Magnetic field lines are closed loops (div B = 0). The flux that goes up through the inside of
the primary must return downward *outside* it (visible in `fig_B_map`). In the plane of the secondary, Bz therefore
points up over the primary and down outside a boundary ring somewhat larger than the primary radius. M is the flux
through the secondary per amp in the primary. As the secondary slides sideways, more of its area lies over the
return-flux region. At the crossing offset the up and down flux through it cancel, so M = 0. Beyond it the net flux
is reversed and M < 0. The boundary ring moves outward with height, because the field spreads further from the coil,
so the crossing moves from 8.2 cm to 10.0 cm.

At the zero crossing no power is transferred, even though the coils still partly overlap. Efficiency and P_L depend
on M^2, so both rise again past the crossing.

## Validation
- dx = 0 reproduces the A-S1 coaxial M exactly (relative difference 0, same function).
- Symmetry: max |M(dx) − M(−dx)| / max|M| ≈ 3e-15 (machine precision).
- max |k| = 0.217 (d = 3 cm), 0.111 (d = 5 cm), so |k| ≤ 1.
- B's `solve()` at f0 equals the closed-form eta along the sweep, including M < 0 (pytest
  `test_circuit_solver_matches_closed_form_along_misalignment`).
