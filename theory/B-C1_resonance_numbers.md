# B-C1: Resonance Numbers, Component Values and Stress Analysis

> **Status:** all currents, voltages, powers and efficiencies below are simulated with
> R1 = R2 = 0.455 Ω, the nominal value of C-T2 (expected range 0.38–0.48 Ω, Section 3; 0.40 Ω before 2026-10-25),
> and the fixed load R_L = 6.0 Ω chosen in C-C1.

## 1. Design Parameters

The wireless power transfer system uses two identical air-core coils
in a series-series resonant configuration.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---:|
| Resonant frequency | f0 | 500 kHz |
| Primary inductance | L1 | 17.13 µH |
| Secondary inductance | L2 | 17.13 µH |
| Primary series resistance | R1 | 0.455 Ω |
| Secondary series resistance | R2 | 0.455 Ω |

The resistance values are modelling estimates (C-T2 nominal, range
0.38–0.48 Ω), not experimentally measured values.

## 2. Resonant Capacitance

For a series resonator, the resonant frequency is

f0 = 1 / (2π√(LC)).

Rearranging gives

C = 1 / ((2πf0)^2 L).

Using f0 = 500 kHz and L1 = L2 = 17.13 µH,

C1 = C2 ≈ 5.915 nF.

A possible practical capacitor combination is

C = 5.6 nF + 330 pF = 5.93 nF.

This combination is a candidate only; actual component availability
and tolerances must be considered. Using 5.93 nF instead of 5.915 nF
will slightly shift the resonant frequency.

## 3. Winding Resistance and Quality Factor

How R is built up (per coil):

| Step | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---:|
| Wire length | ℓ = Σ 2π r_i (10 turns, r = 49.65 … 40.65 mm) | 2.84 m |
| DC resistance | Rdc = ρℓ/(π r_w²), ρ = 1.7×10⁻⁸ Ω·m, r_w = 0.35 mm | 0.125 Ω |
| Skin depth at 500 kHz | δ = √(2/(ωμ₀σ)) | 0.094 mm |
| Skin effect | Rac = Rdc · r_w/(2δ) = Rdc × 1.87 | 0.235 Ω |
| Proximity effect | × 1.5 (assumed; neighbouring turns 0.3 mm apart) | 0.35 Ω |
| Capacitor ESR + joints | + 0.05 Ω (assumed) | 0.40 Ω (first estimate) |
| **Final (C-T2, 2026-10-25)** | skin 0.269 + proximity 0.136 (computed, exact Bessel) + ESR 0.035 + joints 0.015 | **0.455 Ω** |

Limitations of this estimate:

* r_w/(2δ) assumes r_w ≫ δ. Here r_w/δ ≈ 3.7, so it is only approximate
  (the more exact estimate Rac/Rdc ≈ r_w/(2δ) + 1/4 is about 13 % higher).
* The proximity factor 1.5 was an assumption, and it turned out too small: the computed proximity loss
  (`python -m sim.proximity_check`) is 0.136 Ω, about 50 % of the skin-effect resistance.
* C-T2 builds R up term by term: skin effect 0.24–0.28 Ω (including 0.30 m of leads and the
  exact tubular factor), proximity 0.10–0.136 Ω (computed), capacitor ESR 0.03–0.04 Ω (C0G/film),
  joints 0.01–0.02 Ω, radiation about 10⁻⁹ Ω. The total is 0.38–0.48 Ω with a nominal 0.455 Ω,
  which replaced the first estimate above. R is still the least certain input, so B-V1 shows a band
  for R = 0.38 to 0.48 Ω.

The unloaded resonator quality factor is

Q = ω0 L / R = 2π f0 L / R.

Substituting the design values,

Q = 2π(500 × 10^3)(17.13 × 10^-6) / 0.455

Q ≈ 118.3 (112–142 over the C-T2 range of R).

The approximate bandwidth is

BW ≈ f0 / Q

BW ≈ 500000 / 118.3

BW ≈ 4.23 kHz (3.53–4.46 kHz over the C-T2 range of R).

These are estimates based on the nominal resistance. The loaded
bandwidth of the coupled system also depends on the load and coupling.

## 5. Capacitor Voltage Stress

For a capacitor in a series resonator, |VC| = |I| / (ω0 C) = ω0 L |I| (equal to the coil voltage
at resonance; ω0 L = 53.8 Ω here).

The 5 cm operating point above gives VC1 = 44.8 V peak, but this is not the worst case.
The primary current RISES as the coils separate, because the reflected resistance
ω0²M²/(R2 + RL) falls. With the secondary removed, I1 = Vs/(R1 + Rs) is limited only by R1 (+ Rs).

Generated by `python -m sim.stress` (`data/stress_table.md`; all columns, including RMS currents and
R1 loss, in `data/stress_table.csv`). Conditions: Vs = 5 V peak, f0 = 500 kHz, RL = 6.0 Ω,
R1 = R2 = 0.455 Ω.

| Case | I1, Rs = 0 Ω (A pk) | VC1 (V pk) | VC2 (V pk) | I1, Rs = 50 Ω (A pk) | VC1 (V pk) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 cm | 0.04 | 2.3 | 9.7 | 0.03 | 1.6 |
| 3 cm | 0.23 | 12.4 | 22.5 | 0.07 | 3.8 |
| 5 cm | 0.83 | 44.8 | 41.6 | 0.09 | 4.8 |
| 10 cm | 5.84 | 314.4 | 78.3 | 0.10 | 5.3 |
| 15 cm | 9.78 | 526.1 | 49.2 | 0.10 | 5.3 |
| no secondary | 10.99 | 591.4 | 0.0 | 0.10 | 5.3 |

Worst cases from the script:

| Source | max VC1 | max VC2 | max capacitor current | max loss in R1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Ideal, Rs = 0 Ω | 591.4 V pk (no secondary) | 78.3 V pk (10 cm) | 7.77 A rms (C1, no secondary) | 27.5 W |
| Generator, Rs = 50 Ω | 5.3 V pk | 7.4 V pk (2 cm) | 0.10 A rms | 2 mW |

Preliminary component requirements (voltage rating at least 2 × worst-case peak voltage):

* Ideal 5 V source: C1 would need a rating of at least about 1.2 kV and must carry 7.8 A rms at 500 kHz,
  while R1 dissipates up to 27.5 W. This is not practical. A real driver must limit the primary current,
  for example with a current-limited supply or a series resistance.
  The secondary capacitor needs at least about 160 V (a 200 V or 250 V part).
* 50 Ω function generator: VC1 ≤ 5.3 V and VC2 ≤ 7.4 V peak, about 0.1 A rms, so a 50 V C0G/NP0 part is
  more than enough. The price is much less load power (tens of mW instead of watts; see `fig_PL_vs_d`).
* Voltage rating alone is not sufficient. At 500 kHz the capacitor must also handle the RMS current
  without overheating. Check the datasheet's AC/ripple-current rating and its loss (ESR = tan δ/(ωC),
  dissipation ½|I|²·ESR), and the resulting temperature rise. Use C0G/NP0 ceramic or polypropylene film,
  not X7R/Y5V. These ratings are preliminary and only as good as the estimate of R.

## 6. Assumptions and Limitations

1. The inductances are design values and have not been experimentally
   measured.
2. The series resistances are modelling estimates (C-T2: 0.38–0.48 Ω, nominal 0.455 Ω).
3. The capacitances are calculated values; practical component
   tolerances can shift the resonant frequency.
4. The voltage, current, power, and efficiency results are predictions
   from the circuit model.
5. No physical hardware validation is claimed.
6. Stress values assume an ideal sinusoidal source; the driver's own current limit, if any, sets the real maximum.
7. The parameters should be updated if the project adopts different
   final design values.
